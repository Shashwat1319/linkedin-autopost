The 404 page is your most honest page.

Every site has one. Almost none design it.

Mine took an hour and pays back daily:
→ Clear message: "This page doesn't exist" (no jargon)
→ A way BACK: links to home, projects, resume
→ On-brand — doesn't feel like a system crash
→ Real HTTP status (404, not200 — SEO cares)

Why it matters: broken links happen. Referrals, old posts, typos.
The 404 is often someone's FIRST impression of your work.

A dead end with directions = you cared.
A dead end = you shipped and left.

Go check your own /nonexistent-page right now. What shows up?
